# L5 data audit

Source: `data/tickets.csv` (instructor's synthetic export), 3012 raw rows, 2025-09-01 to 2026-08-31. Generated by `python -m triage audit`.

Label-based numbers in this report use the **training window only**. Test labels stay unseen until L8.

## 1. Which columns exist at submission time?

| Column | Role | At submission? | Feature? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ticket_id | identifier | yes | no | Identifier only; it carries no information about urgency. |
| submitted_at | timestamp | yes | no | Orders tickets and defines the time-based split. Hour and weekday are derived from it. |
| requester_id | group | yes | no | Pseudonymous person ID. Used only to keep a requester inside one CV fold, never as a feature. |
| requester_type | policy_excluded | yes | no | Known at submission, but urgency should depend on the problem, not on who reports it. Kept only for the subgroup check. |
| category | feature | yes | yes | Chosen by the requester on the form. |
| initial_channel | feature | yes | yes | How the ticket arrived. |
| description | feature | yes | yes | The ticket text exactly as submitted. |
| description_length | feature | yes | yes | Characters in the description. |
| attachments_count | feature | yes | yes | Files attached at submission. |
| assigned_priority | post_submission | no | no | Set by staff during triage: it is the decision the model is meant to support. |
| handling_team | post_submission | no | no | Assigned after triage, sometimes after escalation. |
| resolution_hours | post_submission | no | no | Only known once the ticket is closed (empty for open tickets). |
| urgent_label | target | yes | no | Approved label from the review panel (1 = urgent). |

Columns that are not in this policy are excluded automatically (whitelist).

## 3. Leakage scan (train window)

Each column on its own, as a predictor of `urgent_label`. An AUC near 1.0 means the column already contains the answer.

| Column | AUC alone | At submission? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| assigned_priority | 0.926 | no | leak: excluded |
| handling_team | 0.732 | no | leak: excluded |
| resolution_hours | 0.937 | no | leak: excluded |
| description_length | 0.588 | yes | fine |
| attachments_count | 0.518 | yes | fine |

The post-submission columns separate the classes far better than anything known at submission, because staff fill them in *after* reading the ticket. A model using them would look excellent in the lab and be useless on a new ticket.

## 4. Repeated requesters and the split

- 467 requesters; median 4 tickets each, maximum 61.
- 95.0% of validation and test tickets come from requesters who already appear in training.
- A strict requester purge would remove 1562 of 1784 training tickets (87.6%).
- Among the 40 requesters with at least 10 training tickets, the urgent rate varies with a standard deviation of 0.091, against 0.095 expected from chance alone. Requesters do not carry a persistent urgency of their own.

**Decision.** The outer split is by time (train → validation → test), because the model will score future tickets from mostly the same people. Inside training, cross-validation folds are grouped by `requester_id` so that model selection is never judged on a requester it has already seen. `requester_id` is never a feature.
